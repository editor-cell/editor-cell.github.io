The carbon books, reconciled

National inventories and satellite measurements are being read against each other – not as a headline, but as an accounting problem.

Global · 12 March 2026

0:00—0:00
Type size
Type size

In the climate crisis, national greenhouse gas inventories kept by governments serve as official, yet slow-moving ledgers of what is burned and released. 1source 1 Today, however, satellites in orbit measure methane plumes and changes in carbon dioxide, opening a completely independent set of books. Scientists use this data to question the discrepancies between official paper figures and what the atmosphere shows – such as a missing landfill or a revised emission factor. 2source 2 As synthesis reports by the IPCC and NASA unequivocally state, warming is human-driven. 1source 1 3source 3 Reconciling orbital books with state ledgers isn't about declaring a winner; it increases the resolution on exactly which sectors need a closer audit to save the planet.
